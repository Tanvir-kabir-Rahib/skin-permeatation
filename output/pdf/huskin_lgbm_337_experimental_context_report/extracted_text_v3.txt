Technical analysis of conditions, references, and within-compound variability for the LGBM ±0.6 subset.
                        Technical summary
                        The 337 selected measurements represent 171 compounds and 71 reference/DOI pairs. Only 54 compounds have
                        repeated selected measurements; 117 compounds therefore have unknown within-compound variability. Among
                        repeated compounds, 21 span more than 0.5 log10 unit and three exceed 1.0 log10 unit. Their median experimental
                        range is 0.381 log10 unit, versus a median LGBM prediction range of 0.0137. The selected data describe substantial
                        protocol/source heterogeneity that the current model largely cannot vary with.

Experimental variability is concentrated in a minority of repeat-measured compounds
Estradiol, 1-butanol, and caffeine have selected-subset experimental ranges above 1.0 log10 unit; methotrexate
reaches exactly 1.000. For the 54 repeated compounds, the median range is 0.381 and 22 receive an identical LGBM
prediction across all their selected rows. Even the largest prediction range is below 0.1. This gap indicates limited
representation of row-specific experimental context, not proof that any one condition caused the observed
differences.

Missing experimental context limits adjustment for protocol effects
Across 4,718 condition cells, 70.8% are available. The median measurement is missing four of 14 condition fields; only
13 rows report all 14. Storage duration is unavailable in 71.5% of rows, acceptor pH in 58.2%, and donor pH in 54.0%.
Used layer is nearly complete (99.4%) and the neat indicator is complete.

Protocol signatures are completely coupled to literature sources
The citations span 1964–2012. Coverage is not dominated by one paper: the top five references contribute 22.3% of
rows and the top ten contribute 35.9%. However, all 140 recorded condition signatures occur within only one
reference. Consequently, reference/laboratory effects and protocol effects cannot be separated using simple grouped
comparisons in this selected subset.

Scope, data, and definitions
One row is one reported measurement. Compounds are grouped by canonical SMILES. The cohort includes only rows
with absolute LGBM prediction error ≤0.6. Experimental variability is max minus min logKp within a compound; fold
range is 10 raised to that difference. Unknown, empty, N/A, and not-reported values count as unavailable. A condition
context combines all 14 condition fields with reference and DOI.

Methodology
The analysis profiles completeness, citation concentration, repeated-measurement counts, within-compound
experimental and prediction ranges, condition-signature uniqueness, and repeated series within a citation. It preserves
every source measurement and adds annotations rather than averaging measurements. Exact row counts, calculations,
and charts are reproducible in the companion notebook.
Limitations and robustness checks
This subset is selected using experimental logKp, creating outcome-dependent selection bias; it is not an unbiased
validation cohort. Most compounds have only one selected measurement. Conditions are incompletely reported,
several fields co-vary, and condition signatures are nested within references. The pH associations are descriptive, not
causal. Row count, source-row uniqueness, the inclusive ±0.6 rule, 171-compound count, 71-reference count, and
notebook execution were verified.

Recommended next steps
    1. Fit any condition-effect analysis on the full, unfiltered HuSkinDB table, not this outcome-selected subset.
    2. Use a hierarchical model with compound and reference effects before estimating pH, temperature, layer,
       preparation, donor/acceptor medium, or cell-type associations.
    3. Add ionization/pH and other protocol variables to future prediction models; the current LGBM condition input is
       limited to experimental temperature (Texpi).
    4. Treat single-measurement compounds as variability unknown, not variability low.
Further questions
Can the original papers resolve the high missingness in storage duration and pH? Are units and skin-thickness
conventions harmonized across references? Does a full-dataset multilevel model retain the pH patterns after
controlling for compound, reference, skin layer, preparation, and medium?
